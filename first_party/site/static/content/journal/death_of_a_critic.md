The aspiring writer wandered about their house aimlessly. It was clean, perfectly clean, but
nonetheless uninviting. The blinds were all shut tightly, as if trying to erase the world by
pretending it did not exist, and the house plants were dying of thirst. The refrigerator stocked
endless copies of the same two meals, and the art on the walls said "tasteful" but not "personal".
It was a perfect retreat, and that is exactly what the aspiring writer was doing: retreating from
the world. Unfortunately, their solace was coming to an end, as today was April 14, and there was a
deadline looming.

As they wandered throughout their home, their thoughts churned, chaotic, and erratic. Gone was their
past, by virtue of the endless ticking, gone was their present, by virtue of their recent sacking,
and if they were reading the tea leaves correctly, gone was their future, by virtue of their
dwindling savings. Their career had come to a screeching halt, courtesy of a particularly
unempathetic manager, and they were running on borrowed time, not quite fumes, but the last tank of
gas they would likely ever encounter. They made themselves an instant coffee, not cheap enough to
offend the senses, but not good enough to truly satiate. They sat upright before their dining table,
which, like all furniture in the house, was well designed, but ultimately devoid of personal
meaning. Upon the surface: a tax return and a pen.

The aspiring writer thought. What were they to do now? The job market was in shambles, and they had
no particular desire to return to a workforce as toxic as post-ZIRP tech, but they had to do
something with their time, lest they simply drop dead. They picked up the pen, a simple, cheap, blue
ballpoint, and spent a minute just looking at it. They studied the curves of its shape, the
imperfections in the material, and the deep, rich blue ink waiting, no _longing_ to become words.
They almost fell asleep as the pen mesmerised them, until a voice inside them said "Go on, do it,
now is your chance", and without thinking, they flipped the tax return over and began to write:

"The drive to write has been building within me for many years, but a voice has always kept me
silent. This voice is woven deeply into my mind, and I have come to know it as The Critic. It
relentlessly keeps me hidden away with fear and shame, and it has finally severed the last of my
connections to humanity, but while it was busy creating fear and holding me back, I was
painstakingly and mercilessly poisoning its roots. It cost me everything, my job, my friends, my
marriage, but it no longer controls me, so perhaps, this is a new beginning."

The aspiring writer looked at the paragraph, somewhat unable to believe they had written it. The
aspiring writer remembered all the times they had tried to write, to express something joyful, and
how much pain The Critic had shown them. What happened today though? Where did these words come
from, and where was The Critic? Rather than question the miracle, they once again put the pen to the
page:

"I have tried writing so many times, but The Critic always stopped me. It nit-picked every sentence,
every word, and every syllable, and after perfecting those, it tore down my tone, the flow of my
words, and my style. Eventually those too would be perfected, only to meet with The Critic’s most
desperate defence: Appeal to futility. In the past, The Critic would have me believe nothing is
truly worth doing, and everything is ultimately futile, but not today."

In their mind, they saw images of snide laughter, pointed fingers, and hateful comments. The Critic,
slow to rise, had returned, and it wanted nothing more than to stop the writer in their tracks. They
stood, pulled open the curtains, and let the light flow into the room. They returned to the table
and continued to write:

"Today I am resisting those messages, and I am choosing to write not in spite of The Critic, but in
defiance of it. There are words worth publishing, and they do not need to be perfect. The lies of
The Critic have wasted and discarded years of my work; shredded pages, deleted files, and discarded
ideas; notions dismissed, possibilities ignored, and potential squandered. All this because the
Critic needed me to be isolated and alone. Not today though, today I am rewriting the script."

They began to write faster, and faster, and faster as the pen did the work for them:

"If you are reading these words, then The Critic failed today. It fell by the wayside, a victim of
its own failures, and hereon out my life and my publications are within my control. A new chapter is
just beginning, and The Critic is no longer a character in my story. So good riddance Critic, you
were never welcome, and I’m not sad to see you go."

The pen began to run out of ink, and each new word was dimmer than the last, but the aspiring writer
pressed on. They wrote just one more sentence before it died altogether:

"As for you, dear reader, I’ve been waiting to meet you for so very long, so hear me when I say:
Hello, World!"

The pen dried up just as the final point landed on the page, but it didn't matter, the reservoir
within the aspiring writer had been tapped, and it was infinite in size. They stood, surveyed the
house around them, and without hesitation began to make changes. Old art was torn down to make space
for the new, and the plants were given the simple water they needed to survive. A new chapter was
dawning, and it was time for change, but first, there was a tax return to file before the day's end.

Exactly one year later the aspiring writer sat at the same table surrounded by chaos. What was once
a pristine space was now little more than a collection of mismatched and disorganised clutter. The
walls were lined with new art, personal and vibrant, but each piece was discordant and they were all
without harmony. The various house plants, once on the brink of death, were now overgrown and
dishevelled. If the old house were a stark reminder of impersonal perfection, this was the
definition of disorder.

The aspiring writer again sat at their dining table, with their forehead resting against the thick
dark wood. Between the table and their skull was yet another tax return, but this time, they were
not alone. Stacked around them were piles of papers, drafts for stories and manuscripts for essays.
An entire year of writing surrounded them, but they still had nothing to show for it, as nothing was
ready for publication. A collection of pens in various colours were strewn across the table, each
fun to write with, but none had ever felt right. The aspiring writer took a sip of their coffee,
waited for it to soothe their aching mind, and picked up the nearest pen without much care for which
they held. Once again they wrote upon the back of the tax return, but this time it was not joy that
guided them, but exhaustion.

"My drafts have filled my notebook and my desire to publish has reached a boiling point, but none
meet my expectations for quality. Each has substance, but they all need refinement and editorial
work, and there are far too many threads running in parallel. Despite my best intentions, every
attempt at revision creates conflict between my authentic voice and my polished tone, and it seems
impossible to make changes without subtracting from the passion that drives me. My frustration is
building, and as my patience wears thin, a part of me demands a path forward. For some time I
thought it was the critic, but they are gone now, and my words are my own. Perhaps the question to
consider is: What spurs me to refine my work in the first place, and what drives me to publish?

Considering this points towards an answer. While it’s true that my original work holds my rawest
truth, my objective is more than speaking without filtration. My goal is to connect with others,
share meaningful experiences, and find ideas worthy of discussion. I hope to discover novel ways of
being, open doors we aren’t aware of yet, and understand the core of the human condition. This path
is difficult for both reader and writer, so polish before publication is mutually beneficial. I
believe an audience is something to be cherished, and there is a certain respect that must be paid.
Readers such as yourself deserve a carefully crafted piece with refinement and finesse, and placing
the burden of editorial on you to preserve my original voice would be distasteful in my view. It
amounts to little more than personal vanity at the cost of the many, and it's a privilege to have
your time. I owe you the refined, not the raw, but I am failing to deliver."

They paused for a moment and rubbed their eyes. Behind the eyes lived a pain that was not exactly
unwelcome, but was nonetheless a heavy burden. In the space behind their eyes the garden of the soul
was as overgrown as the plants around them, and it was becoming difficult to remain organised. Each
day brought new ideas, and there was never enough time to polish them to the standards of
publication. In pursuit of an exit to the bind, they pushed on with their letter.

"Consideration of this position yields a simple and obvious truth: I must find a way to refine my
work without diluting my message or altering my voice. I cannot accept the dichotomy between quality
and authenticity, and I shall seek ways to have both. That is to say, the tradeoff need not be
intractable. I suspect this skill is a necessary step on the path to success, and balance must be
possible. This ambition leads to a practical solution with three parts: an intention, a technique,
and a decision. Let’s take them in order.

"First: My intention towards you, dear reader, is as follows: I hope to provide you with something
effortless to follow and enjoyable to read. I wish to bear fruit both sweet and sour, but never
toxic, tasteless, or unripe. I seek to touch your heart and fill your mind, yet be gentle and tender
to your eyes and ears. I believe creatures such as us thrive in humility and curiosity, but suffer
in the noise of miscommunication and disparagement, meaning the onus is on me to do the heavy
lifting before revealing my work. That is my intention, and I will do my best to honour it."

They felt a weight begin to lift, and pressed on.

"Second: My technique, which still needs polish, stems from a willingness to find areas of growth,
instead of merely searching for errors. There exists a fine line between critique and criticism, and
choosing to focus on making improvements, not simply fixing errors, will help us greatly. When my
mind goes to worries and mistake hunting, it’s best to step away and try again later. My conscious
drive must be patience and authenticity, never premature publication, and certainly not perfection.
Delay will be my tool of choice, and inaction will be my virtue. I believe this approach will
balance my voice with the quality all readers deserve, and although it’s an arduous task, one which
may take a lifetime to master, such is the case for many worthwhile pursuits."

The weight continued to lift and the ache behind their eyes faded ever so slightly.

Third: My decision, which concerns my trust in our bond. As reader and writer, we are bound together
by monologue with little opportunity for discussion or clarification, particularly if you survive
me. This presents many challenges for communication; however, it also creates an opportunity to
deepen our trust. I have decided to trust in my ability to express myself, trust in your willingness
to consider my true meaning, and trust in our common willingness to relate across great distances. I
find liberty in proceeding under the assumption that perfection is unnecessary for effective
communication, and it creates more room for both of us: a space for me to express my creativity and
a place for you to explore new perspectives unrestricted. I will not allow perfection to become my
jailer, and I will not deny you the opportunity to form your own opinion of my work.

The writer took a deep breath and felt the air fill the depths of their lungs. It's ironic, they
thought, how difficult breathing remains despite a lifetime of practice. They focused their mind and
decided to wrap up.

"I have more to say, but that’s enough for now. I will stand by my intention, continue to refine my
technique, and commit to my decision. My work will flourish in due time, and when that happens, we
can continue our conversation. There is no rush, so until then, take care."

The writer bound the tax return in paper and addressed it to the Department of the Treasury,
Internal Revenue Service, Austin, TX 73301-0045.

They wondered if some auditor somewhere was reading their work. Little did they know, they had
become somewhat of an office joke: who on earth, wondered the auditors, had the audacity, let alone
the time, to file diary entries with the IRS? It achieved its goal regardless, and the thrill of
sending work out into the world was the joy that kept the writer writing. As they left the house to
mail their return, lest they face late filing fees, they made a mental note to begin pruning the
plants and tidying the house. "A tidy house is a tidy mind", they told themselves, all the while
making a mental note to remember that quote if they ever achieved broad literary appeal.

Exactly one year later, the aspiring writer sat at the same table with the same form, yet the house
around them had been transformed. It had all the same boundaries and shapes, for the walls could not
move, but everything from the paint to the plants was different. What once was an overgrown forest
had been tamed, and the walls had been painted a vibrant ocean blue. The artwork on the walls had
been replaced with their own paintings, and while none were particularly good, each was deeply
personal. Nothing about this room was perfect, but it did not need to be, and the aspiring writer
finally felt at ease. Following in their predecessor's footsteps, they retrieved a simple black pen
from their pocket, and began to write.

"Two years ago I wrote that The Critic was dead, but it was a lie. It lives within me still,
jabbing, pecking, and tugging at the threads of my mind with relentless razors of spite, vitriol,
and disappointment. I have tried to suppress it, erase it, and repress it, but nothing works
forever. I wish it would stay away, but it always comes back, and it always has something to say.
When I write an essay, it shows me every imperfection; when I write poetry, it shows me visions of
humiliation; and when I just write without purpose, it stays quiet but lets me know I am skating on
thin ice. It never stops, it is never far from the surface, and it is never satisfied."

They paused and reflected on their journal while the pen slowly dripped onto the page. Momentary
anxiety flooded their mind for the tarnish it brought to the document, but they let it go and
returned to their work.

For a time I thought perhaps the critic was necessary, that it would be my saving grace when my mind
and home were burdened by the weight of unbridled creativity, but that is a trap, and we must not
let the critic return. It clears both beautiful prose and terrible drafts with the same vicious
fervour, and it does not care for the space they leave behind either. It has but one goal,
unattainable perfection, and it will never be satisfied. I want to be free of it, but its own nature
prevents freedom for both of us, for all it has are the chains of perfection.

It is that same perfection that tortures every artist and defiles every artwork. It leers at every
scientist and berates every engineer. It culls every idea with spite and ruins every dream with
fear, yet for all it demands, it gives nothing in return, and would not recognise itself in a mirror
if it were all that was. Perfection destroys everything of value, but for all its horror, it holds
the seed of its own destruction, for nothing can be made to its own standards, itself included, and
in its absence we find a worthy companion: imperfection.

Their mind turned to the world beyond the house they had fled so long ago in retreat, and to the
countless individuals still doing the critic's work, but where it had once seen danger and terror,
it now saw a vast, clumsy choreography of survival.

In the violent world of horror and entropy we inhabit, perfection is a distant ghost, and life does
what it must to survive. Lifeforms such as ours transcend perfection through a billion failures and
a trillion deaths, each adding a little more and discarding everything unnecessary. Every being,
every cell and every network fights to survive, however it can, growing from the innate wisdom of
countless generations while adding its own tiny signature. It is evolution that frees us from the
siren song of perfection, and grants us a gift we should cherish until the end of time: The mistake.
It is the mistake that makes us who we are, and should perfection ever join our chorus of life, it
would find itself woefully inadequate, for in this universe, perfection was never truly in the
cards, and a truly perfect being would not survive for long.

In the absence of perfection a gentler voice finds its way to the surface, speaking not from a place
of condemnation, but from earnest acceptance of all flaws. It cannot be known or spoken, but we all
see it in our dreams, and in rare waking moments, it comes forth from the most obvious place, smiles
briefly, then departs before we have time to respond. It does not judge or want, it does not speak
or act, and it does not wait or wander. It gently sits behind our eyes, forever out of reach, but
never absent or afar. It grows through all adversity but imposes no predefined way of being, giving
all the grace to follow their own way, and gently sowing the seeds of change. You might wonder what
it is, but if you can let go of this puzzle, and learn to accept the imperfect paradox of not
knowing, then you may be surprised to find the answer was with you all along.

The Critic is silent now, for perfection does not warrant a response, and in silence all critics
lose what they need most: engagement. Goodbye for now, Critic, I am sure I will see you again, but
that is all you will receive when you return: Acknowledgement."

Satisfied, they signed the tax return, and left their house to mail it once again to the IRS. Each
step felt lighter than the last, and despite the noise of the critic in the back of their mind, they
paid it no attention. It clawed and fought them as they drove to the post office, but they neither
fought back nor capitulated, instead choosing to simply let the critic be. Unfortunately for the
writer, this was one occasion, perhaps the only occasion, where the critic was on their side, for
the IRS took a dim view of improperly filed taxes.
