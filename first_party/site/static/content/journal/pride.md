The activist rode the Caltrain south from San Francisco. A rainbow bag hung from their hip and their
nails were painted in the colors of pride. Red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet, red, orange,
yellow, green. Nearby a few others followed suit, with their own articles of pride adorning their
bodies, but the activist did not feel particularly united. While their attire shouted self-love from
the mountaintops, their face told a different story, as it hung deep and low, belying the despair
percolating within. As they watched the others laugh, talk, and take selfies to share with others,
they could not avoid the lingering thoughts from the parade they had just visited, and so they
sought refuge in the solitary act of journaling. They pulled a small Lenovo ThinkPad from their
rainbow bag, opened a new note, and let the despair take the wheel. Their note read:

"I went to Pride.

Colour, everywhere.\
Rainbow, everywhere.\
Joy, everywhere.\
Death, everywhere.

Hidden in plain sight."

A part of them told them to stop, for this was surely going to get them cancelled, but they pressed
on, because they knew they were right, and they could not let the suffering of others go unnoticed.
Wasn't that the social contract they were meant to uphold? To speak up for those without a voice?
They continued to write:

"Equal rights!\
2 dollar hotdogs.\
Equal rights!\
Beef and pork. Beef and pork. Beef and pork.\
Equal rights!\
2 dollar hotdogs.

The dissonance strikes like a hammer."

It was not just a metaphor, their mind felt battered on all sides, caught between the expectation
for joy, the requirement to perform, and the reality of what they had seen. They pressed on:

"A man wears a t-shirt with a slogan:\
Human kind. Red.\
Human kind. Orange.\
Human kind. Yellow.\
Human kind. Green.\
Human kind. Blue.\
Human kind. Violet.\
Human... kind?

Only one colour matches the blood on our hands."

Their mind filled with images of blood running through the streets, of rainbow painted children
wading in a deep red pool with no awareness of the world around them. Their attention turned again
to the others on the train, and what they might think. Would they hear the message, or simply cast
them out. They tried to continue their writing, but could not. "Homophobic!?" screeched a voice in
the back of their mind, but no, they reminded themself, just not blind. Their words flowed again:

"One eye open.\
One eye asleep.\
One injustice seen.\
One injustice enjoyed.

The endless missing people... the endless dead people...

Find the lives, who were never alive.\
Find the remains, who never remain.\
Find the people, who were never people.

Owned.\
Sold.\
Killed.\
Sold."

They paused, feeling overwhelmed, before grounding against the rough plastic of the chair and the
stready rocking of the train. They pushed on, and let the despair continue to flow into their words:

"A group passes by.\
A flag stands proud.\
A pink triangle shouts.

Never again, it boasts.\
Never again, it claims.

Never again? It's here right now.

Millions proud.\
Trillions dead. A holocaust that never ends."

Their mind filled with rows upon rows of cells, endless cells, with incomprehensible torture within,
no one doing anything to stop it, and no one even bothering to care. They looked out the window, and
could not see it, but they knew it was not far, for it never ended, and it never slowed. They turned
their attention back to their document and put the finishing touches on the poem.

"Who still suffers while we stand proud?\
Who still dies so we can live?\
Who still hurts at every moment?

Not us, no, not us.

We go to pride We go to pride... But human pride is no longer enough."

They saved their note, closed their laptop, and let the thoughts go, but they could not truly part,
and they remained alive in the hollow behind the activist's eyes. The train carried on its way,
creaking and groaning as it followed the tracks, and slowly all others disembarked, until only the
Activist remained. It felt soothing, calming even, to be alone, for this train had a long way to go,
and they were not interested in the company of those who rode halfway.
