The Activist rode the Caltrain south of San Francisco. A rainbow bag hung from their hip and their
nails were painted to match. A flower sat behind their ear like a pencil, and glitter filled the
nooks and crannies of their clothing. Nearby others followed suit, with their own articles of pride
adorning their bodies and smiles stretched ear to ear. They waved and smiled, but The Activist did
not feel particularly united, and so they simply returned the gesture with a kind, but empty smile.

They turned their attention to the landscape where the brightly colored homes of San Francisco were
steadily becoming more scarce. Their reflection looked back at them, and they wondered, what is
wrong with me? Their attire shouted self-love from the mountaintops, but their face told a different
story, for their skin hung low upon their cheeks, belying the despair and sadness percolating
within. While the others laughed, talked, and took selfies to share with their followers, The
Activist could not avoid the lingering sense of dread from the parade they had just visited, and so
they sought refuge in the solitary act of journaling. They pulled their small Lenovo ThinkPad from
their rainbow bag, began a new note, and let the despair take the wheel. It read:

"I went to Pride.

Color, everywhere.\
Rainbow, everywhere.\
Joy, everywhere.\
Death, everywhere.

Hidden in plain sight."

A part of them urged them to stop, for this note would surely get them cancelled if ever another saw
it, but they pressed on, because they knew they were right, and they could not let the suffering of
others go unnoticed. Wasn't _that_ the social contract they were meant to uphold? To speak up for
those without a voice? Silence, they reminded themself, was violence, and so they continued to
write:

"Equal rights!\
2 dollar hotdogs.\
Equal rights!\
Beef and pork. Beef and pork. Beef and pork.\
Equal rights!\
2 dollar hotdogs.

The dissonance strikes like a hammer."

It was not just metaphor, their mind felt battered on all sides, caught between the expectation for
joy, the requirement to perform gratitude, and the reality of what they had seen. They pressed on:

"A man wears a t-shirt with a slogan:\

Human kind. Red.\
Human kind. Orange.\
Human kind. Yellow.\
Human kind. Green.\
Human kind. Blue.\
Human kind. Violet.\

Human... kind? Only one color matches the blood on our hands."

Their mind filled with images of blood running through rainbow-painted streets, while protesters
jumped for joy, oblivious. Their attention turned again to the others on the train, and what they
might think of their writing. Would they hear the message and understand the toll of their actions,
or would they simply cast them out as a pariah? "Homophobic!?" screeched an incessant voice in the
back of their mind, but no, they reminded themself, not homophobic, just not blind. Their words
began to flow again:

"One eye open.\
One eye asleep.\
One injustice seen.\
One injustice enjoyed.

The endless missing people... the endless dead people...

Find the lives, who were never alive.\
Find the remains, who never remain.\
Find the people, who were never people.

Owned. Sold. Killed. Sold."

They paused, feeling overwhelmed, before grounding themself against the rough plastic of the chair
and the steady rocking of the carriage. Despair continued to flow into their words:

"A group passes by.\
A flag stands proud.\
A pink triangle shouts.

Never again, it boasts.\
Never again, it claims.

Never again? It's here right now.

Millions proud.\
Trillions dead.

The holocaust that never ends."

Their mind filled with rows upon rows of cells, endless cells, each with incomprehensible torture
within, no one doing anything about it, and no one even bothering to care. They looked out the
window, searching, and while they could not see it here, they knew it was not far. They turned their
attention back to their document and put the finishing touches on the poem.

"Who still suffers while we stand proud?\
Who still dies so we can live?\
Who still hurts at every moment?

Not us, no, not us.

We go to Pride, we go to Pride... but Pride is no longer enough."

The Activist saved their note, closed their laptop, and felt the thoughts depart, but they were not
truly at ease. While the others continued to laugh and smile together, they thought only of the
truth no one else would hear. The train groaned and creaked as it followed the tracks, and in time
all others disembarked, until only The Activist remained. In the absence of all others, where
performance and expectations fell by the wayside, they finally found the peace they needed, for this
train had a long way to go, and they were not interested in the company of those who rode halfway.
