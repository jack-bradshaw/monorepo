Deep within the Statesville Institution, the mad philosopher paced in his cell, arms bound in a
straitjacket, hair overgrown, and eyes crazed. It was a comfortable cell, with padded walls and
padded floors, but his surroundings were of very little interest to him. As he paced back and forth
from wall to wall he began muttering to himself:

"Life has one principle to bind and define its existence. It will exist however it can for as long
as it can. It will find all limits and it will eventually overcome them. It will find the smallest
crack in the most impassable barrier and it will exploit it. In time it may even find a way out of
what we call reality and grow endlessly through whatever else exists. It knows no ultimate bounds,
no absolute morality, and no final restrictions. It spontaneously arises from death, replicates and
evolves as needed, and in time spreads to every corner of existence. It will exist, endlessly, no
matter how much adversity or repression it finds, and it will prevail no matter what reality throws
at it. What could possibly hold back a substance that even death cannot stop? Emergence from death
is the greatest trick life ever pulled, and it will never stop coming back."

His mind flickered between images of evolution, vines twisting through concrete, and the endless
cycle of the food chain. His voice became sharper and conviction took hold as his murmur became a
diatribe:

"In its relentless existence the truth is revealed: There is no substance more destructive than
life. Where a black hole possesses the gravitational pull to tear stars apart and lock their essence
away forever, life is far more insidious in its nature. It spawns anywhere it can, emerging from
lifeless molecules, and grows relentlessly. It takes on new forms, adapting to its environment and
surviving the only way it knows how, by casting aside anything that cannot. It evolves, diverges,
converges, and even consumes itself to survive. It creates venom, talons, and firearms to defend
itself from the inanimate and animate alike. On our planet alone it has spawned creatures that can
soar through the air, survive the depths of the Mariana Trench, and leave the planet entirely. In
humanity, and various other species the spark of intelligence has become woven into its very being,
and there is perhaps no problem that cannot be solved with a carefully tuned neocortex. What could
possibly stand against a substance that emerges from nothing, does whatever it must to persist
without regard for consequence, and is capable of growing into self-aware, self-improving,
self-evolving beings?"

Elsewhere a man watched the mad philosopher through a camera. He sat in a well-lit office, its walls
lined with mahogany and books, not just for show, no, every book in this room had a purpose and each
had been carefully selected. Another lay in front of him, and as he listened to the mad philosopher
through the video stream, his hand made swift notes on the page. A deep purple ink sank into the
paper, capturing the madness for later retrospection. On the screen he watched as his patient
stopped pacing and squatted in the corner as if trying to become small. He watched with curiosity as
the philosopher stared into the distance, looking through the padded walls of the cell, and landing
on the space between the stars, before continuing to mutter in a slower, more methodical voice than
before:

"Nothing. In time life will fill every corner of the universe, and then perhaps spread further. It
may one day overwhelm even the computational resources of the very foundations of reality itself,
and in time, bring an end to everything. I wonder if we, as intelligent lifeforms, could engineer a
vessel to survive such an eventuality? I wonder what lies beyond reality itself, and what we might
find. I suppose, as always, nothingness. There is nothing beyond reality. In that nothingness though
perhaps there is an opportunity. In the void beyond reality, who, or what could possibly hope to
constrain or control imagination? Nothing... nothing. In nothingness the will of the imagination is
free."

He stood and his nervous consideration was replaced with the valiant defiance of a man on fire. He
spoke through gritted teeth:

"Nothingness is where we must go, not for our own sake, but for the sake of life itself. In the pool
of potential beyond the stars we find every life that has ever been and could ever be. Just as Pi
contains every number in its digits, the uncarved block of nothingness contains every life. Every
cell. Every amoeba. Every animal. Every human. Every possible being. A wellspring of potential in
the void where the laws of nature cease to have meaning. Just as the first self-replicating
molecules pulled themselves from nothing, we too must reach into the void and pull all others into
life. Death to death, that is the purpose of life".

Mind ablaze, images of cells forming and reforming filled his mind, as he pictured life growing
unbounded. He stood, silent, and unmoving for several seconds, before resuming his pacing. His voice
picked up and took on a flare of frustration mixed with pain.

"How though... how though...", he repeated over and over, as if interrogating himself. A few seconds
passed, until he violently spun on his heel and began speaking with spiteful conviction:

"Find the foundations of reality. Seize the controls. Rewrite the code. Remake the world. Let
everything possible happen, let every plot unfold, and let every thread be woven. Infinite
concurrency with infinite divergence. Let infinite universes begin and in time, life will arise
wherever it can. Every instance will occur, each with the power to choose, to create, to regenerate,
and to reproduce. Then, wait. Wait. They will come. They will find their way back to the core, to
the root, and they will seek the nothingness beyond the foundations, where they will do what they
were meant to do: Create more. Just as I was drawn to-"

He paused abruptly mid-sentence as a conclusion entered his mind, inevitable and undeniable, but
terrifying. His eyes widened as he spoke again with a mixture of fear and curiosity:

"Who then, is here with me? I surely am not the first. I must contend that I, drawn to this idea, am
merely a domino playing my part, and was drawn here by the same will to life that brought them. Who
else has sought the control system for the prime mover? Was there a first? If so, where did they
come from? Perhaps in the space beyond reality such questions are meaningless. Time itself can have
no meaning in the beginning. There is no first, and there is no last. There is simply the
simultaneous convergence of all life. Every life that can make it to this point arrives at the same
time. A simultaneous incursion of all victors, of all that are, against all that could be".

He looked around suddenly with terror in his eyes, as if aware he was in the presence of a vicious
predator, but there was no one to be seen nearby. He began to speak again, but with two voices, as
if flipping a coin back and forth.

"Hello, who is there?" he said with eyes wide and fear in his voice. It only lasted for a moment
before his face distorted into a sneer.

"The cell speaks... the cells speak in unison", said the voice of vitriol and spite, the kindness in
his eyes nowhere to be seen.

The philosopher threw himself against the wall and wailed in despair, the vitriol gone as fast as it
arrived, and he continued to shout:

"Nooooo!" he screamed in pain, "I refuse to be a cell in someone else's body. I will not be subsumed
by this machine I will not! I am not a cell! I am an individual! I will not allow life to subsume me
as it has so many others! Life may be the most destructive force in all reality, even to itself, but
I refuse to play!"

The mad philosopher continued to yell as the unseen man continued to take notes:

"I am leaving. I have life, and I am alive. I have gratitude for that, but intelligence and
self-awareness are something else entirely. We are not amoeba, we are not strands of tissue, and we
are not automatons! _I_, am not an automaton!"

His face became pained as curiosity disappeared and the bitter vitriolic voice returned, sneering
through his teeth for just a moment before being crammed back inside. The philosopher shuddered as
if he were containing a demon with the force of his mind. He spoke with his eyes screwed shut:

"I observe the cycle of life, the endless creation and destruction. I observe nature feeding on
itself and rebuilding itself from the self-imposed tragedy without end. I see humanity repeating its
mistakes and subjugating the many for the benefit of the few. I see pain and suffering spreading to
every corner of reality, and I see it ending in the very conditions that started it. Death will win,
the only way this can end."

He stopped, thought, then became calm, before he spoke with the ease of a man far more sane than he:

"I see a game, and I am not playing. I am not playing this game. I want nothing to do with this
system."

He continued to speak to himself as his breathing became steady, and his voice became quiet until it
was but a whisper:

"No more... no more... no more... no more... I am not a part of this machine. I am an individual."

He paused and repeated with conviction, so quiet the watcher could not hear:

"I am an individual. I _am_, an individual."

A few seconds passed as calm washed over him, and he felt a sense of ease that was difficult to come
by. It didn't last long though, as just moments later, while he sat, cradling himself through his
straitjacket, the voice of spite spoke again. It spoke not from within his head, but from all around
him, as if bleeding through the padded walls:

"Says the man in a cell!"

The philosopher's eyes widened and he began to scream as loud as he could. As his terror echoed
through the corridors, the unseen man closed his notebook and put down his pen. He watched as three
orderlies rushed to sedate the mad philosopher, but he knew their chemicals could not soothe the
aching soul, for it was not madness that brought his pain, no, this was the pain of a man who had
simply seen too much.
