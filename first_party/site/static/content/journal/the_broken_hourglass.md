The timeless man walked through the streets of New York City, mumbling to himself.

"Sand falls through the hourglass. It happens whether we like it or not, but have you ever wondered
what makes it move? Do you ever notice yourself in one moment, then another, then another? Try to
slow it down and you can't. Like a passenger on a train watching the scenery pass by, the moment is
not ours, it is simply passing by."

All around him, buildings flickered in and out of existence. The old was replaced by the new in the
blink of an eye, and decay came and went in a heartbeat. Lamp posts oscillated between paint and
rust, trash bags appeared and disappeared, and the people moved through him as if they were ghosts.
The sun and the moon flickered across the sky as day and night battled for the present, and the
cracks in the concrete flowed across the ground like a river. The seasons could not decide which
should stay, and he felt the warmth of summer meet the chill of winter all at once. Meet the man
beyond time, or as he was once known to his friends and family: Garry.

As Garry shifted back and forth through time, all that remained constant was the air within his
lungs. No matter how far he drifted, how many centuries he travelled through, the air was the
constant. Breathe in, breathe out. He continued to speak, not to anyone in particular, but simply as
a way to keep track of himself:

"I need you to understand that my mind works in unusual ways. For me, time passes more like a stream
than a moment, and parts of me move at different speeds. I can't remember how it happened, I was
crossing the road, then there was a flash, and when I came to, I was like this."

He looked down at his hands and watched as they flickered in and out of existence. His entire body
shimmered constantly against the backdrop of the city, never quite present, never quite absent. He
watched as a horse-drawn carriage disappeared and a yellow taxi cab took its place, before
continuing his thought:

"One part of me lives in the far future. Decades from now. Another lives a few moments away, just
past the present. Then there's the part of me in the past, two parts really. One looking back with
purpose, and one trapped, endlessly falling backwards, then bouncing back to the future without
will. We exist together, and we exist separately. We are one being working across time for each
other. The present itself has lost all meaning for me, and all that exists now is the era."

He stopped at a crosswalk. The traffic lights cycled violently: red, yellow, green, red, yellow,
green, creating a strobe effect that would have blinded a normal man. Garry just watched the blur of
headlights streak past him until the car disappeared all together. He crossed their path, and as
they returned, they moved right through him without coming into contact.

He bent down and touched the pavement, but his hand moved right through it.

"Even the ground below my feet is a lie", he said as he began to sink through it, before pulling
himself forward like a man stuck in a bog, "it's only the motion that keeps me from falling
through."

He continued onwards, for how long no one could truly say, and he watched the towers fall, rebuild
themselves, and fall again. As he watched the planes vanish into the distance he spoke again:

"How can one mind be in so many places?", he asked the empty air. "It's tiring and truly exhausting
to be split into so many pieces. Never quite present, and always working for a future that never
quite arrives, all the while being stuck in a past that should never have happened. Splintered,
perhaps, for lack of a better term."

He reached out and touched a brick wall. To a normal person, it was solid. To Garry, he could feel
the clay being baked, the mortar drying, and the inevitable crumble into dust all at once.

"To exist in this shape is painful, and it would be nice to let it go, but I have learned there is a
purpose to all this."

He pulled his hand back and shoved it into his coat pocket, his tone shifting from melancholic to
deeply analytical:

"Beyond time exists a place where repetition is the norm. There are no coincidences here and there
are no random events, everything that happened was driven by something else, and in my experience
these events tend to form loops. I've seen the rise and fall of civilisation, the death spiral that
comes after the bloom, and the inevitable rebirth when all who remember are dead. By understanding
time, and learning about the cycles, we can understand frequency, and find an exit to the repetitive
cycle of human existence. The alternative is undesirable to say the least."

The flickering sky suddenly halted. For one terrifying second, everything stopped. The sun burned a
deep, violent crimson, and the city stood in dead silence. Garry looked up, his eyes reflecting the
bloody hue. An explosion in the distance ripped through the city, not nuclear, no, far more
devastating, for it didn't simply destroy, it erased. It spread outwards like a plague, killing
every cell it touched, and erasing every molecule it encountered. Not even dust was left behind. It
swallowed the world and Garry was left in total darkness, the lone human beyond the end of
everything, before suddenly snapping back to the present. Times Square returned, the blur of
vehicles reasserted itself, and the sun resumed its dance with the moon. Garry continued walking and
speaking to himself:

"All this to say, there is a darkness coming. It's a deep murderous rage that will not stop until
every single human being is gone, and when it's done with us, it will keep going until the very
premise of life itself has been extinguished. It will take our loved ones, it will level our cities,
and it will eradicate every last vestige of substance. I know this because it has happened before,
the cycle always repeats, and I have seen it. I can see it. It gets worse every time, and the next
time will be the last. War is coming and it will be the war to end all wars."

The moment passed, and the city was replaced with an empty field. Generations of the Lenape people
lived and died before Garry's eyes, and as he watched, his voice carried a new, urgent weight:

"We survived last time by working as one, by letting go of our differences and forming a union of
humanity that was greater than the sum of its parts. We made sacrifices and lost people along the
way. We even lost parts of ourselves, and we must now work to remember what it means to be human."

He turned his gaze away from the environment, and looked straight ahead, as if seeing the reader
through the fabric of time itself:

"You are one being scattered across a lifetime. This moment is not your moment. The neck is not the
hourglass. They say time and space are the same, and I have seen enough time to know it is true, so
when you see others ask yourself, who are we?"

He flickered, but before he vanished, he said four simple words:

"We are each other."
