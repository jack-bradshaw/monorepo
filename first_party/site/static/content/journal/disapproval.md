The survivor sheltered indoors, weary and unfocused, as a thunderstorm battered their home. A deep
tension ran through their entire body, but not because of the storm; in fact, the storm was all that
soothed their aching soul, that and their dog, who was snuggled upon their lap. The sound of thunder
rang through their home, and they began to meditate in search of peace, as images of the past crept
into the edges of their vision. Deep within their mind a watcher looked on as a child crept through
a dimly lit corridor, searching for a way out. The watcher followed them slowly and spoke in a
dispassionate drone:

"Create the silence. Listen. Do you hear them?"

The thunder continued to bear down on the house, but in the moments between strikes, silence, and
behind the silence, a deafening chorus of whispers. The child followed the voices and wandered about
until it found a dusty room filled with cobwebs and antiques. The watcher followed, always a few
steps behind, and spoke with bated breath:

"Voices whisper in the background.\
Quiet, yet deafening.\
Judging. Leering. Sneering."

There was another thunder clap.

"Disapproving."

The watcher became a shadow as another figure appeared before the child, monstrous in shape and
size, it loomed above them with bitterness and disdain where its face should be. It said nothing,
for its mere presence filled the space with dread and despair, and in the darkness, the watcher
shared its haunted tale:

"Tap. Tap. TAP.\
A hammer strikes the base of the skull from within.\
Gentle. Violent. Silent. Painful.\
Sharp. Solid. Tender. Broken.

Unrelenting."

The child tried to run but could not move. It stood, transfixed and frozen, as the air around it
became cold as ice, while the monster continued to leer and torment the child without a word. The
watcher, out of sight but not out of mind, carried on:

"Numb. All numb.\
Heat it up. Melt the ice.\
Feel... anything?

Feel anything."

Lightning struck and a curtain parted deep within the survivor's mind. As light flooded the hollow
where the child stood, the monster was revealed as nothing more than a coat upon a rack. The child
began to move, relieved, but still frozen to the bone. Tears formed in its eyes while the watcher
spoke again:

"Gasp. Splutter. Inhale.\
Take the breath denied for decades.\
Not with lungs.

Flood the hollow space.\
Choke back tears.\
They come anyway."

The child began to smile.

"A moment of freedom.\
A moment of joy-\
A momen- TAP.

It snaps closed."

Without warning the shutters closed and the room was dark again. The child froze as the watcher
stood in the corner and continued its dull monologue:

"A room? The wrong room.\
A prison? A home?\
Not real.

Very real.

Always real.\
Always wrong.\
Always home.

A prisoner’s home."

Beyond the shutters seasons began to pass in the blink of an eye but the room remained sealed. The
child tried the door, but it would not budge, so they simply laid down upon the ground and closed
their eyes, while the watcher spoke in riddles:

"Decades passed. Never past though.\
Still here. Still there. Still alive.\
Not alive.

Waiting. Still.\
Completely still.\
Nothing to do but wait."

Dust began to gather upon the child as the watcher 

"The clock holds the door shut. Wait for it to open.\
You cannot leave. You will never leave.\

The clock holds the door shut. Wait for it to open.\
The window remains sealed. The view changes. The others smile.\

The clock holds the door shut. Wait for it to open.\
The body ages. Nothing changes.\

The clock does not stop. The clock can never stop. The door opens.\
Open now. Go. Leave."

The child looked at the watcher with confusion. To their surprise the watcher spoke again, this time
with force and fury:

"Leave!"

The child refused to move, for there was nowhere else it knew, so it simply sat, felt the cold bore
into its bones, and waited.

"Leave..." begged the watcher desperately, one last time.

The child looked to the door which had gently swung open, and whispered just one word:

"No."

The storm raged on and the survivor spent the remainder of the day sitting with their dog. As night
approached the rain continued, and they fell asleep to the gentle sound of rain all around them. In
their dreams they saw the child waiting for nothing in particular, and hoped to wake up with a life
worth living, but they knew, for all their wishful thinking, they could never leave this dreadful
place.
