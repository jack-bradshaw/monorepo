"Enter your driver's license to continue"

The Cynical Engineer stood on the side of the road and frowned. Why did they have to enter their
driver's license just to buy a bus ticket? This was getting ridiculous. They stepped away from the
roadside and opened their ride-hailing app. If they couldn't ride public, they would ride private,
but to their dismay, that too would prove difficult.

"Enter your driver's license to continue," the app stated.

The Cynical Engineer rolled their eyes and groaned. Had no one considered the irony of needing a
driver's license to hail an autonomous vehicle?

"Fine, I'll fucking walk!" they snapped aloud. A passerby gave them a funny look and wondered
whether they should say something (they had after all, seen something). The Cynical Engineer started
to walk as Google Maps, the only service that had not asked for ID, guided them home. It was
a three-hour walk, and after two minutes they gave in, uploaded their ID, and hailed a ride.
Just ten minutes later they were seated in their office wondering how on earth the world had gotten
so insane. Screens and other gadgets surrounded them, and a green light blinked as their security
camera monitored the home.

"I am not a criminal," they reminded themself, "this kind of surveillance is draconian, we should-"
their thoughts were interrupted as their dog began licking their face.

"Stop, down boy!" they said in vain as the dog continued to lick, covering their face in every
kind of bacteria known to man, all so it could express its love and taste where its human had been
all day. As The Cynical Engineer stood and reached for treats, they wondered, was that the answer?
Had humanity been going about privacy all wrong for centuries? Maybe...

Thirty minutes later they sat with 10 different context windows open around them. In the 20s the
idea of getting a PhD worth of knowledge in a few minutes would have been unthinkable, but that kind
of knowledge acquisition was commonplace now. In the last half hour they had become an expert on
the history of privacy, the laws surrounding data collection, and behavioral science. It would not
last, for the tradeoff was high-volume-low-retention, but in the few minutes they had left before
the knowledge faded, they hatched a scheme.

"Alright computer, run a simulation. Let's model the Australian government as a primitive resource
optimization engine, and apply classical conditioning with operant conditioning. Could we shape its
behavior to respect the human right to privacy?"

The model thought for a few seconds before responding:

"Enter your driver's license to continue"

The Cynical Engineer almost threw their laptop across the room in frustration.

"Fine I'll do it myself!" they snapped to the surprise of their nearby dog, before going on:

"Alright let's see, when you want to train a beast you don't give it rules and teach it to
speak properly, you guide it, mold it, and reinforce it, until eventually it does what you want.
So, how do we apply this here? Maybe we've been doing it wrong. Maybe making laws to limit the
government was never the right path. If we think of governments as optimization machines, cynically
designed to keep the powerful powerful while providing enough benefit to keep the populace docile,
then they need two things: Money (to do anything) and approval (to stop the riots), so, it's simple:
Every time a government does something the people don't like, they should riot (positive
punishment), and every time it does something they do like, they should become more productive and
pay more taxes (positive reinforcement). Eventually, if the system works, we should end up with a
government that is aligned with the interests of the people. What about the feedback loop though...
the people are being trained too, so they must be taking on the behaviors the government wants them
to perfo-"

Their thoughts were interrupted by a loud knock at the door.

"Coming!" they shouted.

They peered through the peephole to see two men in dark black suits and solid black glasses standing
on their porch.

"Oh crap," they thought, as the door was kicked in, knocking them to the ground. Before they could
stand, a black bag was wrapped over their head and the world went dark. Their knees ached as they
were dragged over the pavement and thrown into the back of what must be a van. The van sped off,
leaving their dog to wonder where their human had gone, and whether anyone would be coming to
provide more treats.

Three hours later, the black bag was finally removed and The Cynical Engineer was greeted by the
cliché setting of a small interrogation room complete with a two-way mirror, an overhead light
obscuring the face of the man sitting across from them, and furniture that clearly said "budget
cuts". It was all very unsettling, but their thoughts were only with their dog, and what might
happen if no one fed them anytime soon.

"Do you know why you're here?" said the agent sitting across from them.

The Cynical Engineer gulped, and responded:

"Ummmm."

The agent took out a pack of gum and began to chew. They blew a bubble, and as it popped, The
Cynical Engineer jumped. What kind of mind games were these people up to, they thought?

The agent produced a dossier and smacked it on the table. Pages slid across the stainless steel desk
and fell to the floor. The agent grumbled, and with a snap of their fingers, an intern scampered
into the room to pick up the pages. The Cynical Engineer smelled Red Bull and wondered why a kindred
soul would possibly work for these fanatics, but their thought was interrupted as the agent snapped
their fingers in front of The Cynical Engineer's face.

"Where were you on August 21st 2035 at precisely 21:22 AEST?" they asked.

The Cynical Engineer squinted. How the fuck were they supposed to know? They didn't have Instagram.

"I uhhhh," was all they said before the agent once again slammed the folder on the desk. This time
it had the desired effect and the pages flowed across the table, before slowly coming to a stop in
front of The Cynical Engineer. A timestamped photograph of them riding a bus sat before them,
showing their precise location at that precise time.

"Did you really think you could ride without a ticket?" the agent asked.

The Cynical Engineer did a double take.

"Is _that_ what this is about? Oh I was sure this was about- never mind," they said without
thinking, before continuing:

"Look I didn't have my driver's license on me, and I really needed to get home to my dog. Surely
you can understand. I have responsib-"

The agent cut them off mid-sentence.

"Case closed. Offender non-compliant. Submit for immediate processing."

The Cynical Engineer tried to object, but moments later the black bag was back, and they were
unceremoniously pushed and shoved through the labyrinth of... wherever it was they were. In the
moments that followed, they were photographed, stripped of their clothing, and dressed in an orange
jumpsuit. A gruff-looking woman with a ponytail read their reduced rights, and led them to an exit.
As they stepped through, the sunlight blinded them, and as their vision returned, they realized:
This was not a prison, there were no bars and fences, this was just the outside. Behind them stood
the habitation dome, a ginormous concrete and steel construction, with not a single window or
port upon it (save for the tiny door they had come through). They had called it home for their
entire life, but in front of them: Open space, vast open space, filled with small buildings, people,
cars, and all other sorts of machines. A man nearby exchanged papers for flowers, and The Cynical
Engineer's mouth fell open as the man walked away without showing his driver's license.

The Cynical Engineer ran after the man, shouting "Wait! Wait!", and to their luck, the man turned
and greeted them with a smile.

"Howdy newcomer," he said, with a chuckle, "what did they get you for?" 

The Cynical Engineer explained their predicament, the unfortunate incident with the driver's
license, and their encounter with the secret police. The man simply nodded, gave them a pat on the
shoulder, said "welcome to the real world", and walked away.

In the coming weeks The Cynical Engineer met many others, learned of the trivial crimes that had
led to their banishment, and came to understand: This place was not a prison at all, for prison is
not where prisoners live, prison is where the law lives, and once enough people become criminals,
it's just called society. As they adapted to the new normal, and made connections without the fear
of reprisal, they found a life in the space beyond the law. They found true happiness in the simple
act of breathing without approval, buying without a trace, and living without care for the
puritanical views of others, yet for all their newfound liberty, a singular drive consumed them:
Reuniting with their dog.

"Please be alright", they prayed each day, as they plotted and planned their heist.
