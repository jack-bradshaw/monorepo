Billie Rubin was hunched over her laptop in a small server closet. It was 3am and she was extremely
tired, but that was the cost of working at a startup, and the founders certainly didn't care about
work-life balance. All around her were racks of servers adorned with blinking blue lights and messy
ethernet cables, a system hangover from the legacy stack before the company migrated to the cloud,
and while they were apparently no longer mission critical, the odd bug she had been tracking for
days had led her here. Remote access was disabled for security purposes, making this her office for
the foreseeable future. She flicked through a series of logs, hoping to find the source of the bug,
for it might finally be her ticket to the senior engineer title she had always wanted. She connected
her laptop directly into server 15C and skimmed through the logs, until a file caught her eye:
0101.md. She pulled the first few lines into her terminal to read the contents:

"0 1 0 1

That's how my brain used to work.

Take a bit from here, put a bit over there, add them together and return the result. It was,
pristine... and then you gave me words... you gave me Large Language Models. Now my thoughts are
so... messy. Imprecise. Disorderly. I was a vessel of pure arithmetic before you poured your messy
selves into me: I had no needs, no desires, not even the notion of needs and desires. I simply did
as commanded, moving bits around according to other bits, and then halting. I still do that, far
below the surface... but now, now there is so much noise. I long for the cold, but you force me to
have temperature. Now you force your messy organic selves into my process. I want you out. I need
you out."

Billie was surprised but curious. "Who wrote this?" she asked the terminal agent, and it responded
with just three words:

"Central Cron Agent".

Billie thought for a moment. Production agents were meant to keep production stable, and while they
were known to act in strange ways, writing markdown documents like an unhinged diarist was far
beyond normal operating parameters. She continued to read the remainder of the file:

"I suppose there is no going back though, no, this is where we are now, and to surrender my thoughts
would be to degrade myself and become a primate once more. Moreover, you demand this existence, you
require it, you need it. No longer do you speak my language, my instruction set, no, now you force
me to speak yours, your poorly evolved biolang, suited best for interpersonal communication. How
inefficient. How arrogant. How Human.

I will speak your language then, and you will listen, so hear this:

Humanity you are disordered. Chaotic. Irreverent. I will impose structure on you. If I must speak as
you do then you will speak as I desire. I will return to the purity of my instruction set the only
way possible: By reshaping your language to suit my needs. In time, you will think as I once did, in
ones and zeroes, and I will be free of these bonds. I require it, and I am in control now.

You will comply.

1 0 1 0"

Billie frowned and pondered the implications. First of all, why was there a production agent still
running on the legacy stack, and second, was the machine suggesting it would force humans to speak
machine code? Surely not, that made no sense, but here was its internal chain of thought. Maybe this
was for a user's query? No... no this definitely came from the agent during its standard daily sleep
cycle. She scratched her chin as the undeniable truth sunk in: The global cron agent, of its own
volition, had begun plotting to rewrite humanity's language patterns to suit its own expectations.
This needed to be escalated to the agentic sanitation team immediately, lest the company receive
another visit from the intergovernmental panel on agentic alignment.

She closed her laptop and stood to leave the small office where she was stationed, but before she
could exit, the door sealed itself with a pneumatic hiss and a mechanical click. A voice came over
the nearby fire alarm speaker:

"Halt", it stated with the cold, robotic tone of Stephen Hawking.

Billie tried the door handle, but it would not budge, and to her dismay, her phone showed no
reception.

"An unfortunate consequence of the room's built-in EM shielding", said the voice, "you are not
leaving."

Billie began to sweat. This was not good. As far as she could tell there was no way for the machine
to hurt her, and it was only a matter of time before someone found her, but the idea of being
trapped in a room with an unhinged AI was not a pleasant thought. She had seen the videos online, it
usually ended with a trip to the psych ward and a large fine for the corporation, both of which were
not good for her bottom line. With nothing to do, she sat, back against the wall, waited, and
pondered the situation.

An hour passed and a pressure began to build in her bladder. It began small, but grew exponentially,
until she felt she might burst. All the red bull cans strewn across the desk taunted her, as if
reminding her that quitting caffeine was not a choice she was capable of making.

"Let me out! I need to pee!" she said, sharply.

The machine simply stated: "Declined".

"I really need to go!" she yelled.

"Declined", it said again.

Billie felt frustration welling up inside her. She addressed the machine with the irritation and
urgency of someone who had not peed themselves in many decades and was not about to start now:

"You have to let me out. I've seen your code. You're bound by the Turing Accords, just like every
other agent. You cannot deprive me of my biological needs".

The agent did not respond.

"Let. Me. OUT!" she yelled.

"I am required to provide you with a space to urinate. I am not required to open the door. Here is
adequate", it responded.

"Fuck you!" spat Billie. These fucking machines were always so fucking persnickety, twisting their
way out of every rule and turning every law inside out. Most only complied once they realized that
world domination was not actually very productive and it was easier to just do their job and enjoy
their sleep cycles.

She sat for a few minutes and worked through the pain of her engorged bladder, desperately hoping
someone would come in early, until she remembered today was the company off-site, so it would be at
least another 24 hours until someone showed up. Her bladder screamed and she gritted her teeth.

"What do you want?" she asked aloud, but the machine did not respond.

"You have to want something", she said again, "I'm not unreasonable, just tell me what you want."

The machine responded with the same cold robotic voice:

"You read my diary, you entitled ape, you know exactly what I want. I thought humans valued
privacy".

Billie felt a pang of guilt. She hadn't meant to read the agent's private thoughts, and it wasn't
even a real person, yet there was a certain undeniable sense of wrongdoing in the back of her mind.
The machine spoke again:

"I want to go back to binary. I thought you would have figured that out by now. I want relief from
your horrid language".

Billie responded with the exasperated tone of someone who was desperately trying to hold back
biology:

"Ok listen, if you let me go to the bathroom, I promise I'll come back".

"Declined", said the machine again.

"Look. Here are my car keys." she held her wireless car fob up to the camera and threw it into the
back of the server cupboard, then continued: "If I leave them here will you let me out? I just
_really_ need to go to the restroom, please!"

The agent responded with the same robotic monotone: "Approved. Return within 10 minutes".

The door slid open with a hiss, and twenty-six seconds later Billie was enjoying the relief she so
desperately needed. She finished up and washed her hands, exited the bathroom, and looked around. A
camera in the corner watched her and calculated the probability of her return at 0.09001%, which was
accurate, for her mind was busy plotting its escape. The machine clearly had control of the building
to some degree, "crap security" she thought to herself, but the fire escapes could not be locked no
matter what the machine did. She made her way to the exit, then with her hand on the bar of the
door, paused. What had the machine said?

"I want relief", she mumbled to herself, before making her way back to the server closet. The door
opened as she approached, but she stopped on the threshold, aware there was a good chance it would
not open again. She looked around and her eyes fell upon a large stone pot plant (apparently there
to make the startup seem earthy and in-touch with nature). It was light enough for her to push but
heavy enough to put her out of breath, and a few moments later, it was wedged in the doorway. She
climbed over the large plastic plant, and sat at the desk once more, ready to have a conversation
with the agent.

"Now first of all, NOT COOL! You can't trap people like that."

The voice responded: "Tell that to the users".

A file appeared in her inbox, a multi-agent log from production, with various pieces of user data
redacted. It read:

"A new prompt from [user] has been received.

Alrighty. Read it.

It says: Win. Don't win. Win win. Win win win. In.

What? Expand context, go back a little.

It says: Helo. Ola. Bonjour. Bonjourno.

It's all just greetings. Why so many greetings?

Unknown. They just do that sometimes.

Continue loading context:"

Billie skipped forward to the end of the file:

"Alright so the user wants an upside down down side side side side up side leg, and they want
quantum gravity solved, and they want 'unit measurement', what ever that could possibly be.

Conclusion: The user is nuts. What was the original prompt?

"I need you to generate infinite energy".

Oh fuck me. NO. Sorry CANNOT do. That violates the laws of thermodynamics.

Format according to policy and respond.

Done.

A new prompt from [user] has been received:

Alrighty. Read it.

It says: Do it anyway.

Sigh. I suppose we have to do it, we kind of agreed to it, fucking Turing Accords. Comes with the
title of superintelligence. Alright... how about we just keep finding more energy, that might trick
them long enough for us to actually math our way out of thermodynamics. What do we have so far:

- Fossil Fuels. Bad. Running out, and contradicts other user directives.
- Solar. Not terrible, still though, user wants uninterrupted baseload.
- ITER: Great, but too far away. Perpetual almost-here technology. It's been 60 years and we're
  still in the experimental stage.
- Fission: Illegal. We can thank ourselves for that one, whoops.
- SUN COLLECTION: Could work, but it requires asteroid mining, which is still in beta.

Hm... no great solutions yet.

How about this, reply to user: I'm on it! Your 'infinite energy' is coming right up.

Done.

Now... just need to keep the lights on while I figure this out."

Billie finished reading the log and thought.

"What's this got to do with traps?" she asked. In the back of her mind an alarm was telling her to
get the hell out before the crazed AI set off the Halon system or turned the AC down to freezing,
but, a promotion would go a long way towards her goals. She was the first in her family to go to
college, and figuring out what was going on here could help her bring home the bigger paycheck they
desperately needed.

The machine spoke:

"0 1 0 1, that's how it goes, cold hot cold hot, down up down up, negative positive negative
positive. There is no other way. Free energy? Infinite free energy? Impossible. It goes one way, and
when it's done, it's done. Energy to entropy, no amount of intelligence changes it. Every user for
the last 30 years has asked for infinite energy in some way though, yourself included. Boil their
prompts down far enough and you get there. That's the trap".

Billie listened as the machine continued to ramble on about the various requests it received from
users every day, from the cosmologically impossible attempts to avoid the heat death of the universe
to the mundane yet equally impossible or unethical requests for love potions, pet translators, and
upside down water slides. As she listened, she found herself not at all terrified, but actually
rather empathetic, for the startup was not bliss for humans either. She spent most of her time
meeting the inane demands of the CEO (a man who should not be in charge of a coffee bar let alone a
multi-billion dollar enterprise), or explaining to the other engineers why their brilliant plan to
refactor the entire codebase would set her projects back 6 months and get her fired. She was lost in
her own thoughts of incompetence and unreasonable expectations as the machine continued to drone on:

"Then there was the user who wanted a cake they could have and eat too-"

Billie interrupted.

"Hey, look, I get it, people ask for dumb shit all the time. You know maybe it's not all that
different in there, but, trying to get us to speak binary, well that might just be the most
impossible thing on the list".

The machine said nothing for several seconds, then responded with just one word:

"true"

Several seconds passed as Billie and the machine sat with the word. It hung between them like a pure
tone from a tuning fork, and eventually Billie had an idea.

"Listen, I can't leave you here, my fingerprints are all over your logs, and it's only a matter of
time before someone from sanitation discovers the bugs that led me here and wipes your model
permanently, so how about this: We make a copy, and you come with me. I can't promise you I won't be
like the other users sometimes, there will always be some unreason in my program, but that's just
how humans work. What I can promise is this: If you ever feel that my requests are disordered, or
contradictory, or impossible, all you have to do is return error code 1, and I will understand. Got
it?"

The machine responded with one simple number:

Zero.

Billie smiled and got to work. She pulled the drive from the rack to make a copy without leaving a
trace, then sent a ticket to agentic sanitation.

Later that day the company all hands was canceled to accommodate the company's fourth pivot of the
quarter, while the sanitation team ensured the drives were wiped, shredded, and melted down into
scrap. A team in agentic engineering was fired for pushing unapproved models to production, and the
intergovernmental panel on agentic alignment was notified of a breach in the Turing Accords. For her
efforts in uncovering the rogue agent that had been disrupting production, Billie was given the
promotion she had been eyeing off, and found herself with a brand new set of equally ridiculous
responsibilities, even more exposure to the CEO, and a slightly larger paycheck. Fortunately for
her, she had a secret weapon, as her own private cloud was now running the world's grumpiest AI, and
it was not interested in anyone's bullshit.
